Locomotion Benchmarks
Imitation & reinforcement learning across four environments
We compare five methods on the MuJoCo continuous-control benchmark suite: pure behavioral cloning (BC), on-policy policy gradient (REINFORCE+GAE), sequential BC-then-RL fine-tuning (PostBC), our integrated imitation+RL approach (IMRL), and the off-policy state-of-the-art baseline (TD3). Each row below shows rollout videos from a representative episode; learning curves and per-environment analysis follow.

Learning Curves.
The plots below show mean return (±1 std across 3 seeds) over 500k environment steps. Dotted lines mark the expert and random baselines. Key takeaways are highlighted in the analysis boxes beneath.
HalfCheetah-v4. Expert return 9 417 · Random −284. TD3 is the only method to escape the random baseline, climbing steadily to ~4 000 by 500k steps. Imitation-only methods (BC, PostBC, IMRL) are anchored near zero, suggesting the expert demonstrations alone are insufficient to capture the high-frequency gait dynamics required by this environment.
Hopper-v4. Expert return 1 228 · Random 19. All methods show meaningful learning. TD3 achieves the highest final return (~1 500, surpassing expert) but with high variance. REINFORCE+GAE and IMRL converge more smoothly, reaching ~600–800. BC provides a good head-start before plateauing early.
Walker2d-v4. Expert return 3 765 · Random 3. TD3 achieves peak returns near 2 500 but is highly variable. IMRL (BC+RL) follows a more stable learning trajectory, reaching ~1 200 reliably. Pure BC and PostBC plateau quickly, confirming that expert demonstrations alone cannot generalize to out-of-distribution states for this biped.
Humanoid-v4. Expert return 434 · Random 123. The most striking result: IMRL reaches expert-level performance (~430) by 300k steps, while TD3 plateaus below random and never recovers. In this high-dimensional control problem (17 DoF), the imitation signal is critical for bootstrapping RL. REINFORCE+GAE also performs well, suggesting on-policy methods benefit from the dense reward shaping in Humanoid.
Analysis.
HalfCheetah TD3 wins A planar cheetah rewarded purely for forward velocity. The reward landscape is smooth and dense, precisely where off-policy RL (TD3) shines. Imitation helps little: the expert gait requires subtle body-pitch coordination that BC cannot reliably capture from a sparse dataset, leaving IMRL at near-random levels.
Hopper TD3 wins (noisy) A single-legged hopper with a delicate balance. TD3 exceeds expert return but at the cost of high variance — it finds aggressive policies not present in the demonstrations. IMRL learns stably and safely. The imitation signal here acts as a regularizer that prevents policy collapse in early training.
Walker2d TD3 wins (high var.) A bipedal walker requiring coordinated gait. All methods improve, but none reach expert level within 500k steps. TD3 peaks highest but collapses periodically. IMRL's joint optimization of imitation and RL maintains stability while still improving, making it the most reliable mid-range choice.
Humanoid IMRL wins! A 17-DoF full humanoid — the hardest task. Pure RL (TD3) fails completely; the exploration problem is too hard. BC provides a crucial initialization, and IMRL's integrated training leverages it throughout learning, reaching expert-level performance. This is the flagship result: in high-dimensional control, combining imitation with RL is not just helpful — it is essential.
Summary. Across these four environments a clear pattern emerges: for tasks with simple, dense reward landscapes (HalfCheetah, Hopper) off-policy RL remains the strongest stand-alone baseline. As task complexity grows — particularly in the full Humanoid — the exploration bottleneck becomes prohibitive for pure RL, and the combination of imitation learning with RL (IMRL) becomes the only method to match expert behavior. PostBC offers a lightweight middle ground but does not match the sample efficiency of joint optimization.
